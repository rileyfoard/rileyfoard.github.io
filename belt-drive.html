Two-Stage Belt Drive Transmission

  • Gear ratio optimization
  • Belt tensioning
  • Press fits and bearings
  • ME14, team of 5

For Caltech’s ME14 competition, our team had ten days and a $200 budget to build a two-stage belt-drive transmission that could spin a bicycle wheel as fast as possible. Everything had to fit within a fixed mounting area, so choosing pulley ratios was only half the problem.

I performed the belt-tension calculations and optimized the transmission ratios, balancing wheel acceleration against maximum speed. I also drilled mounting holes and helped fabricate and assemble the drivetrain, including press-fitting components and checking alignment and belt tension.

Our wheel reached 250 RPM in six seconds—the fastest time to that mark in the competition—and achieved a top speed of 476 RPM, the fourth highest. It was a satisfying project because we could directly compare our calculations to how the transmission performed under load.
